# Texas course-planner research: primary sources (checked 2026-09-25)

**Scope:** This covers Texas high school graduation rules (the Foundation High School Program), automatic and university admission, state aid tied to courses, and dual credit. All sources were checked on **2026-09-25**. Each fact cites a source ID from the table at the end. A few facts that are only confirmed by a secondary source are marked ⚠️.

**Source hygiene notes:**
- The official text of the Texas Administrative Code (TAC) now lives on the Secretary of State "Appian" portal. The links below use its `recordId` addresses.
- The Cornell LII copies of the Coordinating Board rules are **out of date** (for example, it shows the 2019 version of §4.85). Don't use them.
- Statute text was pulled from the Legislature's file server (`tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/ED/htm/…`). The citations use the public `statutes.capitol.texas.gov` addresses.
- Agency abbreviations: TEA is the Texas Education Agency (K–12). THECB is the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board. SBOE is the State Board of Education. TEC is the Texas Education Code.

## 1. Foundation High School Program (FHSP): 19 TAC Chapter 74, Subchapter B

### 1.1 Program structure

- Every student entering grade 9 in 2014–15 or later graduates under the FHSP. To graduate, a student needs [S1 §74.11(a)]:
  - the FHSP courses;
  - the required end-of-course (EOC) tests;
  - a speech proficiency shown in grade 8 or higher.
- **The FHSP takes 22 credits** [S1 §74.12(a)]. **An endorsement takes 26 credits** [S1 §74.13(c)].
- **Choosing an endorsement:**
  - On entering grade 9, the student must name in writing the endorsement they intend to earn [S1 §74.13(a); S13 TEC §28.025(b)].
  - The student can switch at any time and can take courses from more than one endorsement before junior year [S1 §74.13(b)].
- **Opting out of an endorsement:** a student can graduate FHSP-only, with no endorsement, only after the sophomore year. This needs counselor advice plus a parent's written permission on a TEA form [S1 §74.11(f); S10].
- **Personal graduation plan:** a counselor reviews options with each grade 9 student and a parent, and both sign the plan before the end of that year. The plan can be amended later, and the school must notify parents when it is [S13 TEC §28.02121(c),(f)].
- The transcript must show the Distinguished Level of Achievement (DLA), each endorsement and each performance acknowledgment [S1 §74.11(d)].
- **Other graduation requirements:**
  - **FAFSA/TASFA:** every grade 12 student must file a FAFSA (federal aid form) or TASFA (Texas aid form, for students who can't use the FAFSA), or opt out on a TEA form [S1 §74.11(b)].
  - **Direct-admission election (new):** beginning with students enrolled in 2026–27, each student must elect before graduating whether THECB may share their records for the state direct-admission program. They do this through a My Texas Future profile, or opt out with a parent form, a form signed by an 18-year-old, or counselor authorization [S1 §74.11(c); S13 TEC §28.0257 (SB 2314, 2025); S10 has the opt-out form, dated 08-2026].
- **Course credit rules:**
  - A course credit needs a grade of **70 or higher** [S3 §74.26(c)].
  - High school courses taken in grades 7–8 earn state graduation credit and go on the high school transcript [S3 §74.26(b)].
  - A student can't take a course with a required prerequisite unless they completed the prerequisite or the district finds they have equivalent knowledge [S1 §74.11(k)].
- **Completing a college core curriculum:** a student who completes a Texas public college's core curriculum (TEC §61.822, certified by the college) is treated as having earned an endorsement and the DLA, and is entitled to a diploma [S1 §74.11(q); S13 TEC §28.025(b-7)].
- **AP Computer Science A or IB Computer Science HL** counts as both one advanced math credit and one foreign-language (LOTE) credit [S1 §74.11(n)].

The 4th credit comes from a list: English IV, Independent Study, Literary Genres, Creative Writing, Research & Technical Writing, Humanities, Public Speaking III, Communication Applications (only as a half credit paired with another listed half credit), Debate III, journalism III courses, AP/IB English, Business English, a college-prep English course (TEC §28.014), or a locally developed course after English III. ESOL I/II can replace English I/II for students still learning English. |

- TEA's Sept 17, 2026 letter adds details on the new cohort [S6]:
  - Applied PFL is expected in 2028–29.
  - The new **Foundations in Economics course won't be in classrooms until 2033–34**. Until then, the one-credit economics option can be met with AP Microeconomics or Macroeconomics, or dual credit economics.
- The statute behind the new cohort's social studies is TEC §28.025(b-1)(4) [S13].

### 1.3 Endorsements [S1 §74.13]

**Every endorsement requires, on top of the FHSP:**
- a **4th math credit from the restricted list in §74.13(e)(2)**. This is the List B set above. List A courses such as Math Models do **not** count as the 4th math.
- a **4th science credit** from §74.13(e)(6).
- **2 more elective credits.**
- A student pursuing Arts & Humanities can, with parent permission, swap the 4th science for an ELA, social studies, LOTE or fine arts course [§74.13(e)(6)(X); S13 TEC §28.025(c-3)].
- A course used in an endorsement's four-course set can also count toward FHSP credits and electives, and toward more than one endorsement [§74.13(g)].
- Districts define "advanced courses" and "coherent sequences" locally [§74.13(d)].
- A district must offer at least one endorsement. If it offers only one, it must be Multidisciplinary [S13 TEC §28.025(c-4)].

**The rules depend on when the student entered high school.** Students who entered in 2022–23 or later use these:

- **STEM** [§74.13(f)(6)]: requires Algebra II, Chemistry, and Physics (or Physics for Engineering), **plus one of:**
  - (A) become a career and technical education (CTE) "completer" in a STEM program of study: biomedical sciences, civil engineering, cybersecurity, electrical engineering, engineering foundations, geospatial engineering and land surveying, mechanical and aerospace engineering, networking systems, nursing science, programming and software development, renewable energy, robotics and automation, or web development;
  - (B) Algebra II plus 2 more math courses that require Algebra II;
  - (C) Chemistry, Physics, and 2 more sciences;
  - (D) one more post–Algebra II math course and one more science.
- **Business & Industry** [§74.13(f)(7)]: one of:
  - be a CTE completer in one of 39 listed programs of study (accounting through welding);
  - be a completer in the engineering or IT programs listed above if the STEM math and science requirements aren't met;
  - 4 English credits that include 3 levels of Public Speaking, Debate, Advanced Broadcast Journalism, or Advanced Journalism (Newspaper, Yearbook or Literary Magazine).
- **Public Services** [§74.13(f)(8)]: one of:
  - be a CTE completer in one of 13 programs: biomedical science or nursing science if the STEM requirements aren't met, diagnostic and therapeutic services, early learning, exercise science, family and community services, fire science, government and public administration, health and wellness, health informatics, law enforcement, legal studies, teaching and training;
  - 4 JROTC courses.
- **Arts & Humanities** [§74.13(f)(4), no cohort split]: one of:
  - 5 social studies credits;
  - 4 levels of one LOTE language;
  - 2 levels each of two LOTE languages;
  - 4 levels of ASL;
  - 4 credits in a coherent sequence from 1–2 fine arts disciplines;
  - "four English credits by selecting from" English IV, Independent Study, Literary Genres, Creative Writing, Research & Technical Writing, Humanities, Communication Applications, AP Literature, AP Language, or IB English courses. ⚠️ See flag 14.
- **Multidisciplinary Studies** [§74.13(f)(5)]: one of:
  - 4 advanced courses within one endorsement area or across areas;
  - 4 credits in each of the 4 core subjects, including Chemistry and/or Physics and English IV (or AP/IB English);
  - 4 AP, IB or dual credit courses in English, math, science, social studies, economics, LOTE or fine arts.
- Students who entered high school **before 2022–23** follow the older STEM, Business & Industry and Public Services rules in §74.13(f)(1)–(3), which use generic "coherent CTE sequence" options.

### 1.4 Distinguished Level of Achievement (DLA)

- **The DLA requires:** the FHSP, plus at least one endorsement, plus **4 math credits including Algebra II**, plus 4 science credits [S1 §74.11(g); S13 TEC §28.025(b-15)].
  - An endorsement alone does not guarantee the DLA. Only STEM *requires* Algebra II; the other endorsements only require a 4th math course.
- **Why the DLA matters:**
  - It is the curriculum requirement for **top-10% automatic admission** (§2.1).
  - It is also the curriculum requirement for Texas State's assured admission [S48].
- **Required Algebra II notice (SB 232):** every Sept 1, districts must tell parents of students in grade 9 and up that Algebra II isn't required to graduate, and explain what skipping it means for automatic admission, the TEXAS Grant and TEOG [S13 TEC §28.02123; S9]. ⚠️ See flag 5.
- **Other ways to count as DLA:**
  - A Texas First early-graduation diploma counts as the DLA [S13 TEC §28.0253(f)].
  - So does completing a college's core curriculum (§1.1).

### 1.5 Performance acknowledgments [S1 §74.14, amended effective 2026-08-01]

- **Dual credit:** at least 12 college hours with a 3.0 or higher, or an associate degree while in high school.
- **Bilingualism and biliteracy:** all ELA requirements with an 80+ average, plus one of:
  - 3 credits of one LOTE with 80+;
  - Level IV+ proficiency;
  - 3 core-subject credits taught in another language;
  - AP LOTE exam 3+, IB HL LOTE exam 4+, or "Intermediate High" on a national proficiency test.
  - Emergent bilingual students must also have exited their bilingual/ESL program and scored Advanced High on TELPAS (the state English-proficiency test).
- **AP/IB:** AP exam 3+ or IB exam 4+.
- **Tests:** any one of:
  - PSAT/NMSQT or PSAT 10 National Merit Commended or higher, or College Board National Recognition Program;
  - ACT Aspire composite 442+;
  - PreACT 29+;
  - CLT10 101+;
  - **SAT 1340+**;
  - **ACT composite 29+** (excluding writing);
  - CLT 96+.
- **Business/industry certification or license:** a qualifying credential that is age-appropriate, exam-based and represents substantial skills.

### 1.6 Grades 7–8 (the product serves middle school)

- **Advanced math:** each district runs a middle school advanced math program designed so students can take **Algebra I in grade 8**. Sixth graders are **automatically enrolled** if they scored at or above the 60th percentile statewide on the grade 5 math test, or were in the top 40% on a local measure. Parents can opt out and must be notified at least 14 days before school starts. Districts may also enroll students first in grade 7 or 8 [S13 TEC §28.029; S5 §74.2101].
- **Algebra I and Geometry together:** districts may let students take them concurrently [S13 TEC §28.025(b-6)].
- **Fine arts:** each student completes one fine arts course in grade 6, 7 or 8 [S2 §74.3(a)(2)].

### 1.7 Courses every district must offer (useful as the generic fallback catalog) [S2 §74.3(b)(2)]

- **ELA:** English I–IV plus at least 1 advanced English course.
- **Math:** Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, Precalculus, Math Models with Applications.
- **Science:** IPC, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, plus at least 2 more sciences from a list.
- **Social studies:** U.S. History since 1877, World History, U.S. Government, World Geography, Personal Financial Literacy, Economics, PFL & Economics.
- **PE:** 2 PE courses.
- **Fine arts:** courses in at least 2 fine arts areas.
- **CTE:** 3 or more CTE courses (4+ credits, including an advanced course) aligned to TEA programs of study. The number of programs scales with enrollment: 1 program under 500 students, up to 6 over 10,000.
- **LOTE:** levels I–III of one language.
- **Computer science:** 1 course.
- **Speech:** Communication Applications.
- Small districts can apply for reduced offerings [§74.3(b)(3)].
- **Schedule rules** [§74.3(b)(5)]:
  - A course required for graduation must be taught at least once every two consecutive years.
  - If a course is offered only every other year, students must be told.

### 1.8 GPA weighting (new) [S4 §74.3001, effective 2026-09-23; S7; S13 TEC §28.0252 as amended by SB 1191]

- **Who it applies to:** students who **enter grade 7 in 2027–28 or later**.
- **Rules for these cohorts:**
  - Each district must adopt a GPA policy in which AP, IB, OnRamps dual enrollment, and **academic** (non-WECM) dual credit all get **equal** extra weight.
  - **Workforce (WECM) dual credit gets less** weight than those.
  - Honors weight is set locally.
  - A cohort's policy is frozen through graduation.
- **Current students:** weighting stays local. Districts aren't required to weight honors courses [S3 §74.30(c)].

### 1.9 EOC testing (context)

- The graduation-required end-of-course list in statute is now **Algebra I, Biology, English I and U.S. History**, as amended by HB 8 (89th Legislature, 2nd special session, effective 2025-12-04) [S17 TEC §39.023(c), §39.025]. ⚠️ See flag 10.

## 2. Admissions

### 2.1 Automatic admission: statute and rule

- **Top 10% rule:** every general academic public university **must admit** an applicant who meets all of these [S14 TEC §51.803(a)]:
  - graduated in the **top 10%** of their class in one of the two school years before the year they apply for;
  - graduated from an accredited Texas public or private high school, a Department of Defense school, or a "nontraditional" secondary education such as home school;
  - **either** completed the **DLA** (or an equivalent curriculum at a school not covered by §28.025) **or** reached "a score set by [THECB] on a college entrance examination designated by coordinating board rule." SB 1241 (2025) changed this wording. ⚠️ See flag 1.
- **Valedictorians** are also admitted automatically [§51.803(d-1)].
- **If DLA courses weren't available:** a student who took all the DLA courses available to them counts as meeting the curriculum requirement [§51.803(b); S18 §5.5(c)].
- **Transcript deadline:** by the **end of junior year**, the transcript must show whether the student has completed or is "on schedule" to complete the DLA [§51.803(d); S18 §5.5(b)(2)].
- **Class rank** is measured no earlier than the end of grade 11. It must be reported as a number out of class size, and the top 10% can't hold more than 10% of the class [S18 §5.5(f)].
- **Current rule text for the test-score alternative** (19 TAC §5.5, last amended 2019-08-22) [S18; restated at S29]:
  - meet ACT's College Readiness Benchmarks; **or**
  - on the SAT taken March 2016 or later, score **480 or more in Evidence-Based Reading & Writing and 530 or more in Math**, with no combined minimum.
- **UT Austin cap:** UT Austin may limit automatic admits to **75%** of its first-time resident spaces, admitting by percentile from the top. It must tell districts which percentile applies by Sept 15 each year [§51.803(a-1),(a-2); S18 §5.5(e)].
- **Optional top-25% policy:** each university may adopt a top-25% automatic admission policy, which also requires the DLA or a qualifying test score [§51.804].
- **Everyone else:** to be considered at all, a non-automatic applicant needs the FHSP (an endorsement isn't required) or a qualifying test score. Universities then weigh up to 18 listed factors [§51.805; S18 §5.5(h)].
- **Automatic transfer admission:** a student who qualified as top 10% can later transfer automatically after completing a community college core curriculum with a 2.5+ GPA within the statutory time windows [§51.8035].
- **Direct admissions:** students enter class rank, GPA and SAT/ACT in their My Texas Future profile and can see a list of participating institutions that will admit them "as early as the end of their junior year." THECB reported 31 participating institutions in Nov 2024 [S34]. ⚠️ See flag 15.

### 2.2 University-specific rules

- **UT Austin**
  - **Automatic %:** **Top 5%** for Summer/Fall 2026 and Spring 2027, and **Top 5%** for Summer/Fall 2027 and Spring 2028 [S37; S8, TEA letter dated 2025-12-18].
    - Home-schooled (nontraditional) applicants for Spring/Fall 2027 and Spring 2028 need SAT 1570+ or ACT 36 [S37].
  - **Majors:** automatic admission doesn't guarantee a major. Automatically admitted students who miss their first-choice major may be considered for their second choice [S38].
  - **Tests:** SAT or ACT (or CLT) **required**. ACT Science isn't required [S35]. UT uses the best single test date, with no superscoring [S38].
  - **Course prerequisites (to be competitive)** [S36]:
    - English: 4 required.
    - Math: 3 required, 4 strongly recommended, including Algebra II or higher.
    - Science: 2 required, 4 strongly recommended (biology, chemistry, physics suggested).
    - Social studies: 3 required, 4 recommended.
    - Foreign language: **2 of the same language required**; ASL and computer science count.
    - Recommended: PE 1, fine arts 1, electives 6.
    - Dual credit counts toward these.
  - **Calculus readiness** is required to apply to all Engineering majors, all Geosciences majors, the Environmental Science majors, and Computer Science and its combined majors. Any one of these works [S36]:
    - SAT Math 620+ or ACT Math 26+;
    - AP Calculus 3+;
    - IB HL Math 4+;
    - college Calculus I with a B or better;
    - high school Calculus I with a B or better;
    - CLT Math 26+.
- **Texas A&M**
  - **Automatic:** top 10% plus the Uniform Admission Policy. All Engineering applicants, including top-10% students, are reviewed for placement into General Engineering [S39].
  - **Tests:** "test optional and will not require ACT or SAT scores" [S39; S40].
  - **Recommended courses** [S40]:
    - English: 4.
    - Math: 4, including Algebra I, Algebra II and Geometry.
    - Science: 4, including two of Biology, Chemistry or Physics.
    - Foreign language: 2 of the same language.
    - Engineering applicants are encouraged to take math beyond Precalculus.
- **Texas Tech**
  - **Assured admission by rank and test** [S41]:
    - Top 10%: no minimum.
    - Rest of first quarter: ACT 24 or SAT 1180.
    - Second quarter: 26 or 1240.
    - Third quarter: 27 or 1280.
    - Fourth quarter: application review.
  - Business (Rawls), Engineering (Whitacre), Biology, Cell & Molecular Biology, Microbiology, Physics and Interior Design accept only assured-admission students. Test-optional applicants to those programs are reviewed holistically.
  - **Tests:** test optional.
  - **Recommended curriculum: the DLA**, which Tech notes is required for top-10% admission:
    - English: 4.
    - Math: 4 (Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, one advanced).
    - Science: 4 (Biology, IPC or advanced, two advanced).
    - Social studies: 3.
    - Foreign language: 2 of the same language. ASL is accepted. **CS counts for admission but not for Tech's graduation language requirement.**
    - PE 1, fine arts 1, electives 6.
- **University of Houston**
  - **Assured admission** [S42] with any one of:
    - top 10%;
    - **3.5+ core GPA** (unweighted, computed by UH from English, math, science and social studies);
    - SAT 1250+ or ACT 26+.
  - **Tests:** optional through fall 2030.
  - **Major-specific rules for 2027 terms** [S43]:
    - **Business:** top 5%, 3.9+ core GPA, or SAT 1350+/ACT 29+ with a 3.8+ core GPA.
    - **Engineering:** top 10%, 3.8+, or SAT 1400+/ACT 32+ with a 3.0+.
    - **Natural Sciences & Math:** the same thresholds as Engineering, **plus** coursework: 2 of Biology/Chemistry/Physics, English 4, Algebra 2, Geometry 1, Pre-Calculus 1.
    - **Nursing** (apply as pre-nursing): top 10%, 3.8+, or SAT 1300+/ACT 27+, plus Biology 1, 2 more sciences, Algebra 1, Geometry 1, 1 more math, English 3.
- **UT Dallas**
  - **Holistic review.** Factors include rigor (AP, IB, dual credit), rank, GPA, and **"preparation for … the intended major."**
  - **Automatic:** top 10% plus the DLA.
  - Students who don't get their first-choice major may be offered a different one [S44].
- **UTSA**
  - **Guaranteed admission** [S45]:
    - Top 25%: no minimum.
    - Second 25%: SAT 1170 or ACT 24.
    - Otherwise holistic review.
  - **Tests:** optional.
  - Some majors have extra requirements; students who miss them are admitted to "University College."
  - UTSA's catalog restates the UAP benchmarks as ACT English 18, Reading 22, Math 22, Science 23 [S46].
- **UNT**
  - **Guaranteed admission** (it notes the rules vary by start term) with any one of [S47]:
    - top 30%;
    - unweighted GPA 3.25+;
    - 3.0+ GPA in 12+ hours of dual credit;
    - 3.0+ core GPA with 4 English, 4 math, 4 science and 3 social science;
    - ACT 22+ or SAT 1100+.
  - **Tests:** optional.
- **Texas State**
  - **Assured admission requires the DLA** (or the older Distinguished or Recommended plan, or equivalent) **plus** rank and test [S48]:
    - Top 25%: no minimum.
    - Second quarter: ACT 22 or SAT 1090.
    - Third quarter: 26 or 1250.
    - Fourth quarter: 29 or 1330.
  - Tests are not required for the top 75%. Fourth-quarter students must submit qualifying scores.
  - **Recommended:** English I–IV, 4 math (through advanced math), 4 science (Biology, Chemistry plus 2 advanced), 3 social studies, 2 LOTE.
  - All BBA majors (business) are limited-access.
- **UT Arlington**
  - Guaranteed admission is based on rank, unweighted GPA (3.25+ guarantees), and optional SAT/ACT or "advanced credits" (dual credit, AP, IB, honors) [S49]. ⚠️ See flag 12.
  - **Tests:** optional.

## 3. State aid tied to coursework

### 3.1 TEXAS Grant (public universities)

- **Statute:** for high school graduates on or after May 1, 2013, the academic requirement is completing the FHSP (or equivalent) **plus any two of these four** [S15 TEC §56.3041(a)(2)(A)]:
  - (i) the IB diploma, or 12+ college credit hours earned in high school (AP, IB, dual credit or articulated courses);
  - (ii) meeting the TSI college-readiness standard, or being exempt;
  - (iii) top third of the class, or a GPA of 3.0 or higher;
  - (iv) an advanced math course after Algebra II, **or** an advanced CTE or technology applications course.
- **How it's applied now:**
  - Institutions must give **highest priority** to students meeting those criteria [S15 TEC §56.303(f)].
  - The current rule and FY2027 guidelines treat the FHSP plus two-of-four as a **priority** criterion, not a gate [S26 §22.233(h); S32].
- **New for FY2027 (2026–27)** [S26 §22.233(b),(f); S32]:
  - Priority goes to students in the **top 25%** of their class.
  - Institutions **must** offer a grant to a top-25% student whose Student Aid Index is at or below the priority level (**$6,478** for 2026–27) and who filed a FAFSA or TASFA **by June 1**.
- **Amounts for 2026–27** [S32]:
  - Up to **$5,399 per semester** and **$16,197 per year** (fall, spring and summer).
  - The target amount is $2,500 per semester.
- **Timing and dual credit:**
  - The student must enroll within 16 months of high school graduation [S25 §22.228].
  - "Entering undergraduate" (first 30 college hours) **excludes dual-enrollment hours and credit by exam** [S27 §22.226(1); S32].
  - So dual credit doesn't remove initial eligibility.

### 3.2 TEOG (community colleges)

- The statute has **no high school curriculum requirement** [S15 TEC §56.404]. ⚠️ See flag 5.

### 3.3 Texas First (early graduation)

- **Diploma requirements** (rule effective 2026-02-11) [S24 §4.402]:
  - at least 22 credits;
  - a 3.0 GPA;
  - 80th percentile or higher on the ACT, SAT, PSAT/NMSQT, TSIA/TSIA2 or GED, **or** top 10% of the class;
  - EOCs passed;
  - mastery shown in ELA, math, science, social studies **and LOTE**.
- **Scholarship for 2026–27:** **$10,798** if the student graduates 2+ semesters early, and **$5,399** if less. It must be used by the end of the first academic year after graduation [S32].

### 3.4 Automatic admission and aid notices

- Districts must post and hand out notices about automatic admission and the course requirements for financial aid.
- Top-10% juniors and seniors get individual notice by the 14th day after the fall semester ends [S13 TEC §28.026; S11].

## 4. Dual credit (and AP/IB credit)

- **Counts toward graduation:** dual credit that goes beyond the high school course can satisfy graduation requirements, including advanced courses and endorsement requirements [S1 §74.11(j); S13 TEC §28.025(b-18)].
- **Every district** must offer a way to earn at least **12 college hours** in high school, through AP, IB, dual credit or articulated courses [S13 TEC §28.009(a)].
- **Which courses qualify:** outside early-college programs, academic dual credit must be a college **core curriculum, CTE, or foreign language** course [S13 §28.009(a-4),(a-5); S16 TEC §130.008(a-1); S19 §4.85; S20 §4.83(10)].
- **No state limits:** rules may not limit how many dual credit courses a student takes, and (in one enacted version of the statute) may not limit grade levels [S13 §28.009(b)]. ⚠️ See flag 9.
- **Who is eligible** (rule effective 2024-05-16, amended 2026-05-13) [S19 §4.85(b)]:
  - A student is eligible if they are:
    - **not degree-seeking**;
    - **TSI-exempt**;
    - **TSI college-ready** on the TSIA2;
    - **or** eligible for a Texas First diploma.
  - A non-degree-seeking high school student **cannot be required** to show college readiness [S30; S22 §4.54(e)].
  - Colleges may apply their regular course prerequisites and add requirements that don't conflict with the rule [S19 §4.85(b)(3)-(4)].
  - Public, private, parochial and home-school students get equal access. A student at a school not formally organized as a high school counts as a high school student at age 16+ [S14 TEC §51.9675; S19 §4.85(b)(1)].
- **College Connect courses:** colleges may enroll students who aren't college-ready into math or communications College Connect core courses [S21 §4.86(c)].
- **Degree plan:** a dual credit student must file a degree plan by the end of the second regular semester after reaching **15+ dual credit hours** [S14 TEC §51.9685(c-2)].
- **TSI standards** [S22 §4.54; S23 §4.57]:
  - **TSIA2 cut scores:** Math College Readiness score 950+ (or a diagnostic level of 6). ELAR 945+ with an essay of 5+ (or a diagnostic level of 5–6 with an essay of 5+).
  - **Exemptions (valid 5 years):**
    - SAT EBRW 480 and Math 530;
    - ACT taken on or after 2023-02-15: English + Reading combined 40, and Math 22;
    - STAAR **English III** EOC 4000 and **Algebra II** EOC 4000;
    - a C or better in college-level math, reading or writing, **including dual credit**.
- **FAST program:** dual credit is **free** for students who were "educationally disadvantaged" (eligible for free or reduced-price lunch) at any time in the current or four prior school years, in grades 9–12 at participating colleges [S13 TEC §28.0095; S31].
  - The FY2027 cap on dual credit tuition at participating colleges is **$60.10 per credit hour** [S31].
- **How much college credit transfers in** [S14 TEC §51.968]:
  - Colleges must publish their AP, IB, CLEP and dual credit policies.
  - They **can't require more than a 3 on an AP exam** without evidence it's needed.
  - An IB diploma earns at least 24 hours.
  - Dual credit earns college credit only if it's core, CTE or foreign language (except in early-college programs).

## 5. What this means for the planner (derived from the rules above)

1. **Rules depend on cohort, so the planner needs cohort fields:**
   - grade 9 entry year before or after **2026–27**, which sets the social studies requirement;
   - high school entry before or after **2022–23**, which sets the endorsement options;
   - grade 7 entry in **2027–28 or later**, which switches on the state GPA weighting rule.
2. **Separate "FHSP 3rd math" from "endorsement 4th math."** List A courses (such as Math Models) satisfy the FHSP but not the endorsement's 4th math or the DLA.
3. **DLA = endorsement + Algebra II + 4 math + 4 science.** It unlocks top-10% (UT: top-5%) automatic admission. The transcript must show "on schedule" by the **end of grade 11**, so Algebra II and the endorsement path should be on the plan by then.
4. **TEXAS Grant priority** can be computed from the plan: 12+ dual credit/AP hours, TSI-ready or exempt, top third or 3.0, advanced math after Algebra II or an advanced CTE course. Pick two.
5. **Major prep signals from sources:**
   - UT calculus readiness (Engineering, CS, Geosciences);
   - Texas A&M: math beyond Precalculus for Engineering;
   - UH NSM and Nursing course lists;
   - Tech and UH business/engineering admission floors.
6. **Generic fallback catalog:** use the §74.3 list of courses every district must offer.

## 6. Flags and uncertainties

1. **The automatic-admission test alternative is changing.** SB 1241 (2025) replaced the statute's specific ACT/SAT standard with "a score set by THECB … designated by rule" [S14 §51.803(a)(2)(B), §51.805(a)(2)]. The rule (19 TAC §5.5) **still carries the 2019 text** [S18]. A THECB test-score study was due Aug 1, 2026 [S14 §51.8031]. Expect a rule change; don't hard-code these numbers.
2. **ACT benchmark values** (English 18, Reading 22, Math 22, Science 23) come only from UTSA's catalog [S46]. ACT's own PDF [S50] couldn't be read by machine. The enhanced ACT makes Science optional (UT doesn't require it [S35]). How a missing Science score affects the UAP benchmark is **unknown**.
3. **UNT describes the SAT benchmark as "1090 out of 1600"** [S47]. That conflicts with the rule's section scores of 480 EBRW and 530 Math [S18]. The rule governs.
4. **UT Austin's percentage for Fall 2028** (today's juniors) wasn't published on UT's site or in a TEA letter as of 2026-09-25, although statute requires notice to districts by Sept 15. Re-check.
5. **The Algebra II notice overstates aid consequences.**
   - The required notice says skipping Algebra II affects **TEXAS Grant and TEOG** eligibility [S9; S13 §28.02123].
   - But the TEOG statute has no curriculum requirement [S15 §56.404].
   - For the TEXAS Grant, Algebra II is not strictly required. It matters through the two-of-four priority criteria, where advanced math after Algebra II is one option.
   - **Don't tell families that no Algebra II means no TEXAS Grant.** Say it lowers priority and blocks automatic admission.
6. **TEXAS Grant eligibility vs priority.** The statute frames FHSP plus two-of-four as an eligibility requirement [S15 §56.3041]. The THECB rule and guidelines treat it as **priority** [S26; S32]. The practical effect depends on funding.
7. **THECB has proposed further changes to the dual credit rules** (§§4.81–4.87 and a new §4.88) [S33]. Their text wasn't reviewed. Current §4.85 was last amended effective 2026-05-13 [S19]. §4.85 cites "§4.83(11)" for the dual credit definition, but the definition is now §4.83(10) [S19; S20].
8. **The "at least 16 years of age" wording in §4.85(b)(1) is ambiguous.** The statute applies the 16+ rule only to students at schools not formally organized as high schools, such as home schools [S14 §51.9675; S16 §130.008(e)].
9. **Dual credit grade levels and course limits are set locally in practice.** State rules can't cap them, but colleges can add requirements and district agreements define what's offered. Verify per district and college.
10. **The EOC list changed.** HB 8 (2025, 2nd special session) lists Algebra I, Biology, English I and U.S. History, dropping English II [S17]. The implementation schedule wasn't verified. The Texas First rule still references "English I or II" [S24].
11. **TEA's 2025 Graduation Toolkit** [S12] predates the June 2026 personal-finance change. Only its existence was confirmed; it wasn't read.
12. **University pages often don't state the admission term.** This applies to Texas Tech and UTA, and UNT says only that rules "vary by start term." The **UTA table didn't parse cleanly**: it's unclear whether "advanced credits" (5 for 2nd quartile, 9 for 3rd) is an alternative to or an addition to the test scores. Confirm with UTA.
13. **Test-optional status** (Texas A&M, Tech, UH, UTSA, UNT, UTA) is a per-cycle policy. Re-verify every August.
14. **The Arts & Humanities English option** ("four English credits by selecting from" a list of advanced courses) is ambiguous about whether English I–III count. Confirm with TEA or a counselor.
15. **The direct-admissions institution count** (31, as of Nov 2024) is likely out of date. mytexasfuture.org blocked automated fetches.
16. **Dual credit hours and the TEXAS Grant 150-hour cap** [S15 §56.304(d)]: not verified. THECB says only that dual credit hours "may impact state financial aid" [S30].
17. **Foundations of Economics** appears as an option in §74.12 but won't be in classrooms until 2033–34. In the meantime, AP Micro, AP Macro or dual credit economics count [S6].
18. **The GPA rule (§74.3001) sets the pattern, not the numbers.** Each district sets the actual weight values, so districts still differ.
