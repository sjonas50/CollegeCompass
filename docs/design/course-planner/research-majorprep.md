# Major preparation map: high school coursework by major family, and how much rigor colleges expect

**Status:** research draft for counselor review, not yet reviewed. Compiled 2026-09-25 for the Texas, Tennessee and Utah proof of concept.

**How to read it:**
- **Gates** are rules a college or agency publishes, quoted with a source.
- **Targets** (the math and science goals) are recommendations I put together from the evidence. Each one is tied to its sources. None is a published rule unless it is marked as a gate.
- Where I could not verify a fact (for example age minimums for nurse aides, EMTs or cosmetology), the entry says so. The planner must not state those facts.

## 1. Codes the planner can use

### Math track targets

In a standard sequence of one math course per year (Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, Precalculus, Calculus), calculus by grade 12 requires Algebra I by grade 8. Utah names its integrated courses Secondary Math 1, 2 and 3 ([USBE-CTE]). Another route to calculus is taking two math courses in one year or taking math in summer. This timing rule is sequence arithmetic, not a published rule.

| Code | Target by grade 12 | Minimum that still keeps the path open |
|---|---|---|
| **CALC** | Calculus: AP Calculus AB/BC, IB Math HL/SL, or dual-credit Calculus I | Precalculus, plus proof of math readiness (a test score or placement exam) |
| **PRECALC** | Precalculus or trigonometry. In Utah, concurrent-enrollment Math 1050/1060 | Algebra II, plus a fourth-year math |
| **STATS** | Algebra II plus statistics (AP or dual-credit where offered). Precalculus also works | Algebra II, plus a fourth-year math |
| **ALG2+** | Algebra II plus a fourth-year math the student chooses (statistics, financial math, medical math) | Algebra II |
| **APPLIED** | Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II, with applied math (shop math, blueprint reading) | Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II |

**Texas floor for every family:** a Texas student who skips Algebra II cannot get automatic college admission, the TEXAS Grant or the Texas Educational Opportunity Grant. Districts must send families a notice about this every year ([TEA-GRAD]).

### Evidence grades used in the table

- **A:** a published admission, placement or program requirement.
- **B:** the published first-year curriculum at a target public university. What the first semester assumes shows what to be ready for.
- **C:** government or professional guidance (BLS, FAA, ABET, MIT, Stanford).
- **D:** research literature.

## 3. What colleges expect in rigor, by selectivity

### Evidence

- **What admission offices weigh.** Share of colleges rating each factor of "considerable importance" for first-time freshmen in Fall 2018: grades in all courses 75%, grades in college-prep courses 73%, strength of curriculum 62%, test scores 46%. "Institutions that were more selective placed more emphasis on grades in college prep courses and strength of curriculum." [NACAC]
- **Selective public flagships in the proof-of-concept states:**
  - UT Austin: "students are encouraged to take demanding coursework, going beyond the level required." Its review includes "competitiveness of the major you applied to." Automatic admission is Top 5% for Summer/Fall 2026 and Spring 2027. [UT-PRE] [UT-REV]
  - University of Utah: the main factor is the academic record, with emphasis on "rigorous course selection … (particularly college-level courses such as AP, IB, and dual/concurrent enrollment)," grade trends and unweighted GPA. There is no minimum GPA, but "75% of freshmen admits … had an unweighted high school GPA of 3.5 or higher." [UU-ADM]
  - UTK: 16 core units (4 English, 4 math, 3 science, 2 of one language, 1 arts, 1 U.S. history plus 1 world or European history or geography). In the core GPA, honors adds +0.5 and AP, IB, Cambridge and dual enrollment each add +1.0. "Completion of all sixteen core courses is not required for admission but strongly encouraged." [UTK-FY]
  - Texas A&M recommends 4 English, 4 math (including Algebra I, Algebra II and Geometry), 4 science (two from Biology, Chemistry, Physics) and 2 of one language. [TAMU-HS]
- **Very selective colleges:**
  - Yale: "among the most challenging programs available at my school"; "Increase your rigor throughout high school … senior year program … at least as challenging as their junior year"; "a balanced set of the rigorous classes available to them." [YALE]
  - Stanford: about five academic courses each semester including the final year, and "not necessarily those who take every single AP, IB, Honors, or Accelerated class." [STAN]
  - MIT: "at least some exposure to the foundations of calculus," and introductory physics, chemistry and biology "if available." [MIT]
- **Open-admission colleges:**
  - UVU "is an open-admissions university." [UVU]
  - Texas public colleges place students with the Texas Success Initiative. Students are exempt if they meet benchmarks on the SAT, ACT, or the STAAR English III or Algebra II end-of-course exams, among other routes. Otherwise they take the TSI Assessment, and falling short of a benchmark means "developmental education academic support." [THECB-TSI]
  - The University of Utah places students into math by ACT/SAT, ALEKS, AP/IB or concurrent-enrollment credit. [UU-MATH]
- **Course-taking research (mostly observational):**
  - Rigorous courses are associated with better high school and college outcomes, "often larger for disadvantaged youth" (propensity-matched Florida census). [LCI2012]
  - Math beyond Algebra II is "the tipping point of momentum toward a bachelor's degree." [ADELMAN]
  - "High-school math counts" for college biology, chemistry and physics, while out-of-discipline high school science does not. [ST2007]
  - Mastery of precalculus foundations has "more than double the impact" of taking calculus in high school on college calculus grades; "students with weaker mathematics preparation gained the most from taking high school calculus." [SS2018]
- **AP randomized trials:**
  - AP science raised science skill and interest in a STEM major, but students had "lower confidence …, higher levels of stress, and worse grades." [AP-RCT-JHR]
  - It had no effect on SAT/ACT scores, and it raised aspirations but not enrollment at higher-quality colleges. [AP-RCT-EFP]
- **Dual enrollment:**
  - The What Works Clearinghouse (2017) found positive effects on college degree attainment, college access and enrollment, credit accumulation, high school completion and high school achievement, "with a medium to large extent of evidence." It found no discernible effect on college GPA. [WWC-DE]
  - In the CCRC tracking study (descriptive), 88% of community-college dual-enrollment students continued in college, with large differences by state and by income. [CCRC-DE]
- **Equivalence and caveats:** UTK weights AP, IB, Cambridge and dual enrollment equally, and Utah groups them as "college-level." How credit transfers and applies to a major varies. A large dual-credit total can change a student's admission route (UTK nursing's 45-hour note). [UTK-FY] [UU-ADM] [UTK-NUR] [CS-AP]

### Proposed rigor tiers

The tiers use the same admission-rate cutoffs as `describe.ts` (0.25 and 0.5) so the app stays consistent. Those cutoffs are an app convention, not an external standard. Scorecard `OPENADMP` (column 1774 of the loaded 06102026 file; value 1 means open admission) gives a direct open-admission flag.

| Tier | How to detect | What these colleges expect (sourced) | Planner target (synthesized) | Examples (Scorecard 06102026) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** | `OPENADMP=1`, or no admission rate | High school diploma. Placement decides whether a student starts in credit-bearing math and English (TSI in Texas, ALEKS/ACT in Utah). Health programs have their own prerequisites | State graduation requirements (Algebra II in Texas); reach the placement benchmarks; complete a CTE pathway plus credential for career programs; dual or concurrent credit where it fits | UVU, Weber State, Utah Tech, SLCC, Davis Tech, Austin CC, TSTC, Pellissippi State, TCAT-Knoxville |

**Major-level adjustment (proposal):** raise the tier one step for majors whose published gates exceed the campus rate. Examples are the UT Austin calculus-readiness majors ([UT-PRE]), UTK engineering's SPI rule ([UTK-ENG]), and UTK nursing direct admission ([UTK-NUR]). Scorecard reports one admission rate per campus, so it cannot show this.

**Rigor is relative to what the school offers.** Yale ("available to them"), MIT ("available at your school") and Turning the Tide (access gaps) all judge rigor against the school's own offerings. The planner should define "most rigorous" from the confirmed school course list. A course the school doesn't offer should never count against the student. Dual enrollment or online courses can be shown as options, never as expectations.

## 4. Student wellbeing: rigor versus overload

### Evidence

- Teens aged 13–17 need 8–10 hours of sleep per 24 hours (CDC, citing the AASM). [CDC-SLEEP]
- In the Challenge Success 2011 survey, students taking 5 or more AP or honors courses averaged about 4 hours of homework a night. "Rarely do we see students who can handle 4 or 5 AP courses at once who are still able to participate in extracurricular activities and get the sleep they need, but setting general caps may not work as well as helping each student find the right courses." [CS-AP]
- High school achievement gains from homework appear up to about 2 hours a day, then fade. Among students doing 3 or more hours, 80% cited homework as a major stressor. High schoolers doing more than 3 hours "lost an extra 30 minutes of sleep." This is not a causal finding. [CS-HW] [GCP2013]
- In competitive schools, students showed "high levels of chronic stress, particularly in relation to academic performance and the college admissions process," and some coped with alcohol and drugs. [LEONARD2015]
- Turning the Tide (2016, "endorsed by over 80 key stakeholders in college admissions"), Recommendation 2: "taking large numbers of AP or IB courses per year is often not as valuable as sustained achievement in a limited number of areas … Even the most advanced students may benefit from avoiding course 'overload'." [TTT1]
- Turning the Tide II (2019) asks high schools to "create limits on advanced courses." [TTT2]
- The AP randomized trial found more stress and lower confidence among AP takers. [AP-RCT-JHR]

### Proposed guardrails for the deterministic planner

These are product heuristics drawn from the evidence above, not published thresholds. A counselor should review them.

1. **Never score or rank a plan by how many AP/IB/dual-credit courses it has.** Show fit against the target tier and the family's "rigor first" subjects instead.
2. **Put advanced courses where they matter first.** Suggest college-level courses only in the family's key subjects and the student's own interests. Challenge Success advises enrolling in AP courses "only in areas that are of real interest to you and in which you are prepared." [CS-AP]
3. **Soft load warning, never a block:** warn when a year has 4 or more college-level courses, or when the student reports more than about 3 hours of homework a night. Pair the warning with the 8–10 hour sleep guidance. Let the student set their own maximum, and never auto-suggest above it.
4. **Mastery before acceleration.** Suggest doubling up or skipping ahead in math only if the prior course grade was B or better (the same bar as UT Austin's "B or higher" readiness marker) and the student opts in. Show that precalculus mastery outweighs high school calculus [SS2018]. Remind engineering families that readiness can also be shown by a test score [UT-CR] or placement exam [UU-MATH] [TAMU-CAT-ME].
5. **Senior year:** keep about five academic courses [STAN] [YALE], but don't add new load in grade 12 to catch up. Flag gaps in grade 10 or 11 instead.
6. **Grades 7–8 (some students are under 13):** plan only math placement (when to take Algebra I) and exploration. Utah pathway charts list junior-high courses such as "Exploring Health Science," "Exploring Technology," "Creative Coding" and "Python 1" [USBE-CTE]. No AP-style load.
7. **CTE counts as preparation, not a detour.** A Texas Program of Study can satisfy the STEM endorsement when the math and science requirements are met (Nursing Science, Biomedical) [TEA-POS]. Utah marks many pathway courses as concurrent enrollment [USBE-CTE].
8. **Every plan is labeled a draft to take to the school counselor**, per the owner's decision.
