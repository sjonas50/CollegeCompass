# Tennessee: research for the course planner

**Checked:** 2026-09-25. Every fact below carries a source ID like **[S1]**. The Sources table at the end gives each ID's URL. Every source was checked on 2026-09-25.
**Scope:** Tennessee Department of Education (TDOE) and State Board of Education (SBE) graduation rules, admission course requirements at the public universities, lottery and state scholarships, dual enrollment, and early postsecondary opportunities (EPSOs).
**Legend:** ⚠ means uncertain or conflicting and needs human review before it goes into planner data.

---

## 0. Key rules for the planner

1. **Diploma:** 22 credits minimum. Students who entered 9th grade in 2024-25 or later also need 1 computer science credit, which can stand in for the 4th math, the 3rd science, or an elective-focus credit. Every student must take the ACT or SAT (or another 11th-grade test the Commissioner names) and pass a US civics test. **[S1][S2]**
2. **The diploma already contains the usual university core:** 4 English; Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II plus a 4th math; Biology plus Chemistry or Physics plus a lab science; US History; World History; 2 years of one world language; 1 fine art. **[S1][S2]**
   - **The risk is a waiver.** A director of schools may waive world language or fine arts. The parent must agree in writing. **[S2]**
   - Several public universities require those units (UTC, UT Martin, TSU, APSU). UT Knoxville, MTSU and Memphis recommend them strongly. **[S8][S13][S15][S27][S29]**
3. **Substitutions allowed for the diploma may not count for university admission.** State policy lets computer science stand in for the 4th math or 3rd lab science. It lets Physics or AP Computer Science stand in for the 4th math, and CTE courses such as Floral Design stand in for fine arts. **[S3]**
   - No university source I found says these substitutes satisfy its admission units. UT Knoxville's math list names algebra, geometry, trig, calculus, statistics and other advanced math, not computer science. **[S8]** ⚠ The planner should flag these substitutions for university-bound students and send the question to the school counselor.
4. **Two GPAs matter, and they are computed differently:**
   - **HOPE GPA (the state's Uniform Grading Policy).** Percentage points are added to course grades before the letter is assigned: +3 honors, +4 local or statewide dual credit and industry-certification courses, +5 AP, IB, Cambridge, CLEP and dual enrollment. The GPA is then taken on a straight 4.0 scale. Quality points above 4.0 are not allowed for lottery scholarships. **[S4]**
   - **UT Knoxville core GPA.** The university takes unweighted grades in its 16 core courses and adds 0.5 for honors and 1.0 for AP, IB, Cambridge and dual enrollment. It uses grades through the end of junior year. **[S8][S10]**
5. **Scholarship thresholds (all use the FAFSA):**
   - HOPE: ACT 21, SAT 1060, **or** a 3.0 Uniform Grading Policy GPA. **[S30]**
   - General Assembly Merit Scholarship (GAMS): 3.75 GPA **and** ACT 29 or SAT 1330. **[S32]**
   - Ned McWherter: 3.75 **unweighted** GPA **and** ACT 32 or SAT 1430. **[S34]**
   - ACT superscores are not accepted for HOPE, GAMS or McWherter. **[S30][S32][S34]**
6. **Dual Enrollment Grant:** high school juniors and seniors at 2- and 4-year colleges; any grade 9–12 at a Tennessee College of Applied Technology (TCAT). It pays for up to 10 courses. **[S36][S37]**
7. **Timeline items the state requires** (the High School and Beyond Plan) **[S1]:**
   - career aptitude assessment in grade 7 or 8;
   - plan started in grade 8;
   - career interest inventory by the end of grade 9;
   - **elective focus chosen by the end of grade 10;**
   - in grade 11, the plan extends 2 years past high school.

## 1. Graduation requirements (traditional diploma)

### 1.1 Credits by subject

Sources: SBE High School Policy 2.103 (revised 02/21/2025) **[S1]** and SBE Rule 0520-01-03-.06 (amendments effective 12/24/2025) **[S2]**. The SBE policy index lists 2.103 as last revised 02/21/2025. **[S5]**

| Area | Credits | Required content |
|---|---|---|
| English | 4 | English I, II, III, IV **[S1][S2]** |
| Mathematics | 4 | Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II (or Integrated Math I, II, III) plus a 4th course beyond Algebra I / Integrated Math I. Computer science may be the 4th. **[S1][S2]** |
| Science | 3 | Biology; Chemistry **or** Physics; a 3rd lab science. Computer science may be the 3rd. **[S1][S2]** |
| Social studies | 3 | US History & Geography; World History & Geography; Economics; US Government & Civics **[S1][S2]**. ⚠ Neither text gives the credit split. A 0.5 + 0.5 split for Economics and Government is common but was not confirmed in these sources. |
| Personal Finance | 0.5 | 3 years of JROTC can substitute if the JROTC instructor took the Personal Finance training **[S1]**. (Rule text says "one JROTC course" **[S2]**. Policy 3.103 names JROTC III **[S3]**.) |
| Wellness | 1 | Called "Lifetime Wellness" in the rule. Marching band and athletics may **not** substitute. **[S1][S2]** |
| Physical Education | 0.5 | Can be met by documented equivalent activity: marching band, JROTC, cheerleading, school athletics or intramurals, or other areas the local board approves. **[S1]** |
| World Language | 2 | Both credits in the **same** language. The district may waive it; the rule requires the parent's written agreement. **[S1][S2]** |
| Fine Arts | 1 | Waivable on the same terms as world language. **[S1][S2]** |
| Elective Focus | 3 | CTE, science and math, humanities, fine arts, AP/IB, Cambridge, computer science, or other areas the local board approves. A CTE focus must be 3 credits in the same career cluster or state program of study. **[S1][S2]** |
| **Total** | **22** | **[S1][S2]** |

**Other diploma rules:**
- **Computer science credit (1).** Required for students who entered 9th grade in 2024-25 or later, which is the Class of 2028 onward. It may substitute for only one of: the 4th math, the 3rd science, or elective-focus credit(s). Students who transfer in from out of state or from a nonpublic school in 12th grade are exempt. **[S1][S2]**
- **Math in 3 years.** Students must be enrolled in math (or an approved substitute) in at least 3 years of high school. The rule applies this starting with 2024-25 graduates. A math credit earned in middle school still counts, but the 3-year enrollment rule still applies. **[S1][S2]**
- **Middle school credit.** State Board-required high school credits earned before grade 9 count toward graduation. **[S1][S3]**
- **Credit by exam.** A student may earn up to 4 graduation credits by passing a district-made exam in a course the school offers but the student is not taking. Eligible courses: English IV, Pre-Calculus, Anatomy & Physiology, Ecology, World History & Geography, Economics, Personal Finance, and level I of Latin, French, German, Spanish, Russian, Japanese, Chinese, ASL or another language. The score counts in GPA under local policy. **[S1]**
- **Summer courses.** A first attempt at a course in summer is meant for students on an accelerated path. If the course has an end-of-course (EOC) exam, credit waits until the student takes the EOC in the following fall. **[S1]**
- **Minimum load.** Full-time students in grades 9–12 must be enrolled for at least 5 credits a year. **[S2]**
- **Dual enrollment credit.** Local high schools **shall** accept dual enrollment credits as substitutes for aligned graduation requirements, including general education and elective focus. **[S1][S3]**
- **Elective-focus waiver.** The director of schools may waive the 3rd elective-focus credit in 12th grade if it would delay graduation. **[S1][S2]**

### 1.2 Testing

- **ACT or SAT in 11th grade.** Every student enrolled in a Tennessee public school in 11th grade must take the ACT, the SAT, or another 11th-grade test the Commissioner names, unless medically exempt. It is a diploma requirement. **[S1][S2]**
- **End-of-course exams.** Given in English I, English II, Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, Integrated Math I–III, US History and Biology I. Students taking substitute courses (AP, IB, Cambridge, dual enrollment, dual credit) do not take the EOC. The local board sets the EOC weight in the final grade at 5–15%. **[S1]** ⚠ This is the policy text; check whether any later statute changed the range.
- **US civics test.** The district writes it. Passing is 70% correct, retakes are allowed, and the pass goes on the transcript. It is required to earn the social studies credit. **[S1][S2]**

### 1.3 Approved substitutions (SBE Policy 3.103, revised 02/27/2026) [S3]

- **4th math:** AP, IB or Cambridge math; dual enrollment math; Statewide Dual Credit Pre-Calculus or Statistics; Calculus; Mathematical Reasoning for Decision Making; Precalculus; Statistics.
  - Also listed: Physics I; AP Physics C (both); AP Physics 1 and 2; AP Computer Science Principles and A; many computer science courses (Coding, Cybersecurity, Data Science, Mobile App Development); two agriculture CTE courses; and work-based learning practicums for students pursuing the Industry 4.0 distinction.
- **3rd lab science:** AP, IB or Cambridge science; dual enrollment science; Biology II; Chemistry I and II; Earth & Space; Ecology; Environmental Science; Geology; Anatomy & Physiology; Physical Science; Physical World Concepts; Physics I and II; Scientific Research; computer science courses; many CTE courses (for example Agriscience, BioSTEM I–III, Engineering Design I–II, STEM I–III, Veterinary Science).
- **English III or IV:** AP Language, AP Literature, AP Research, AP Seminar; Cambridge or IB English; dual enrollment English.
- **Economics:** AP Micro or Macro; dual enrollment Business or Economics; CTE courses (Business Economics, Entrepreneurship, and others).
- **US Government & Civics:** AP US Government, AP US History, dual enrollment or statewide dual credit American History, Pre-Law I–III, American Business Legal Systems.
- **Fine arts, CTE substitutes:** Digital Arts & Design I, Floral Design, Foundations of Fashion Design, Foundations of Interior Design, Landscaping & Turf Science, Web Design Foundations.
- **Lifetime Wellness:** JROTC I or basic training. **PE:** JROTC II.
- **Usage limit:** a course may substitute for only one requirement. Exceptions: one full credit may cover two half credits, and a substitute may also count toward the elective focus.
- **Phase-out:** Statewide Dual Credit is being phased out over 3 years starting in 2025-26. **[S3][S6b]**

### 1.4 Honors and distinctions (Rule 0520-01-03-.06(22)–(32)) [S2]

- **State honors:** at or above **all** ACT subject readiness benchmarks, or the SAT equivalent.
- **State distinction:** a B average **plus one** of the following:
  - an industry credential on the state-promoted list;
  - a Governor's School;
  - All-State arts;
  - statewide or national recognition through a student organization;
  - National Merit Semifinalist or Finalist;
  - ACT 31+ (or SAT equivalent);
  - scores of 3+ on at least 2 AP exams;
  - E/e or higher on at least 2 Cambridge A or AS exams;
  - the IB Diploma;
  - 12+ semester hours of postsecondary credit.
- **District distinction:** 3.0 GPA plus a state-promoted industry credential.
- **Tennessee Tri-Star Scholar:** ACT composite 19+ (or SAT equivalent) plus a capstone industry credential, or completion of the Work Ethic Distinction.
- **Seal of Biliteracy:**
  - 3.0 GPA in English courses;
  - English proficiency (for example ACT Reading 22+, SAT reading/writing 480+, or AP English 3+);
  - world language proficiency (for example ACTFL Intermediate-Mid, AP 3+, or IB 4+).
- **Industry 4.0 diploma distinction:**
  - declare intent before the end of 10th grade, with parent consent;
  - enroll in work-based learning or dual enrollment in both 11th and 12th grade;
  - meet a career coach monthly from 11th grade;
  - earn 9 credits of dual enrollment or work-based learning in grades 9–12.

### 1.5 Move on When Ready: early graduation (Policy 2.103 §II) [S1]

**Earn 17 specific credits:**
- English I–IV; Algebra I and II; Geometry;
- US History; 2 credits of one world language;
- 1 of Economics, Government, World Civilizations or World Geography;
- 1 arts course; Wellness; Biology; Chemistry.

**And meet all of the following:**
- a 3.2 GPA (Uniform Grading Policy);
- scores of on-track or mastered on each EOC taken;
- **ACT Math 25 and ACT English 25** (or SAT equivalents);
- a passing score on a world language proficiency test;
- 2 EPSO courses.

The result is a traditional diploma. It brings unconditional entry to a public 2-year college and **conditional** entry to a public 4-year university. A student may take two high school English courses in one year.

### 1.6 Uniform Grading Policy (SBE 3.301, revised 05/19/2026) [S4]

- **Scale for grades from 2022-23 on:** A 90–100, B 80–89, C 70–79, D 60–69, F 0–59.
- **Percentage points added before the letter:**
  - honors +3;
  - local or statewide dual credit and industry-certification-aligned courses +4;
  - AP, Cambridge, CLEP, IB and **dual enrollment +5.** Dual enrollment was +4 in 2022-23 and 2023-24 and is +5 from 2024-25.
- **Exam condition:** weighting for AP, IB, Cambridge, CLEP, dual credit and industry-certification courses depends on sitting the matching exam.
- **Rounding:**
  - course averages of .5 or more round up;
  - GPA is reported to the hundredth;
  - the transcript labels it "Hope Scholarship GPA."
- **Retakes:** a failed first attempt is dropped from the HOPE GPA when the course is repeated. A passed credit recovery course is recorded as 60%.
- **Honors courses:** must go substantially beyond state standards and include at least 5 of the 9 listed components.

### 1.7 Course catalog for the generic fallback

SBE Policy 3.205, Approved High School Courses (revised 5/30/2025), lists every course a Tennessee high school may offer for credit, grouped by area. **[S6b]** It is a good seed for the "generic course types" fallback. It covers:
- **Math:** Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, Integrated Math I–III, Statistics, Precalculus, Calculus, Mathematical Reasoning for Decision Making.
- **Science:** Physical Science, Biology I and II, Anatomy & Physiology, Chemistry I and II, Earth & Space, Geology, Environmental Science, Ecology, Physics I and II, Physical World Concepts, Scientific Research.
- **Social studies:** US History, Economics, US Government, Psychology, Sociology, World Geography, World History, Contemporary Issues, Ancient History, African American History.
- **World languages:** Latin, French, German, Spanish, Russian, Japanese, Chinese, ASL, other languages, levels I–IV.
- **Arts:** music, visual arts, dance, theatre and media arts.
- **Computer science:** Computer Science Foundations, Coding I and II, Cybersecurity, Data Science I–III, and others.
- **Wellness and PE.**

AP, IB, Cambridge and dual enrollment course codes live in TDOE's Course Code Management System. **[S6b]**

### 2.2 Details that affect planning

**UT Knoxville core GPA [S8][S10]**
- Weighted on the 16 core courses: +0.5 honors, +1.0 AP, Cambridge AS/A, IB and dual enrollment.
- Built on unweighted high school grades. If the school doesn't weight, UT doesn't either.
- Uses 6th-semester grades (end of junior year).

**UT Knoxville in-state guaranteed admission [S10]**
- Who qualifies: Tennessee residents at a Tennessee high school who apply by early action (submit by November 1, materials by November 15).
- Test bar: **ACT 24+ (SAT 1160+) with ACT Math 19+ (SAT Math 510+) and ACT English 19+ (SAT reading/writing 500+).**
- Grade bar: **either** a 4.0+ UT core GPA **or** the top 10% of the class. The class must have at least 15 students.
- Admission to the chosen major is not guaranteed.

**UT Knoxville program add-ons [S8]**
- **Engineering:** Success Prediction Indicator = ACT Math + 10 × core GPA. It must be at least 60, with **ACT Math 25 or SAT Math 590.** Students below that are reviewed holistically or can start as University Exploratory.
- **Nursing:** requires test scores and looks at core subjects and GPA.
- **Architecture and design:** portfolio recommended.
- **Music:** audition or interview for some concentrations.

**UT Knoxville deadlines (dates page)**
- Early action: submit by November 1, materials by November 15.
- Regular decision: submit by January 5, materials by January 20.
- Official scores due May 15. **[S12][S8]**

**Tennessee Tech program add-ons**
- Website: Engineering (including Computer Science) needs a 2.85 GPA, ACT 17 and ACT Math 17; Math needs ACT Math 21. **[S22]**
- Catalog: the College of Engineering needs a **3.00 GPA, ACT 20 and ACT Math 22**; the math major needs ACT Math 21. **[S23]** ⚠ These conflict.

**MTSU placement**
- ACT below 19 in reading or math, or below 18 in English, leads to required "prescribed" courses. SAT cutoffs are listed too. **[S17]**

**Memphis guaranteed admission pathways [S26]**
- Taking a University of Memphis dual enrollment course in senior year.
- Completing all four "Tiger Prep" dual enrollment courses (select high schools).
- Taking part in "One Step Closer."

**TN Direct Admissions**
- Every eligible Tennessee Promise applicant in the Class of 2027 receives a Direct Admissions letter. It lists the colleges that have already accepted the student and estimated aid at each. **[S39]**
- MTSU says it participates. **[S17]**

## 4. Dual enrollment and early postsecondary opportunities

### 4.1 What counts as an EPSO

TDOE recognizes these EPSO types [S7]:
- Advanced Placement;
- Cambridge International;
- CLEP;
- Dual Enrollment;
- International Baccalaureate;
- Local Dual Credit;
- Statewide Dual Credit;
- department-promoted industry credentials.

Definitions (Policy 3.301 §III) [S4]:
- **Dual enrollment:** taught by postsecondary faculty, for both college and high school credit. On a college campus, at the high school, or online. Weighting applies if the student passes.
- **Statewide dual credit:** a high school course aligned to a challenge exam. Passing earns credit at every Tennessee public college or university. Weighting requires sitting the exam. **Being phased out over 3 years from 2025-26.** **[S3][S6b]**
- **Local dual credit:** challenge exam under an agreement with one college. Credit is good only there.
- **AP, IB, Cambridge, CLEP:** weighted if the student sits the exam.

Other effects:
- Students in substitute courses (AP, IB, Cambridge, dual enrollment, dual credit) skip the matching EOC. **[S1]**
- School accountability for 2026-27 counts a graduate as "college and career ready" for any of: ACT 21 or SAT 1060; ASVAB AFQT 31+; a Tier 3 industry credential (or Tier 2 plus one more); or **one or more postsecondary credits.** **[S7]** This explains why schools push EPSOs.
- ⚠ TDOE's EPSO pages now redirect to futureready.tnedu.gov, which renders only in JavaScript and could not be read. **[S42]**

### 4.2 Dual Enrollment Grant (2026-27) [S36][S37]

- **Eligibility:**
  - 2- or 4-year colleges: high school **junior or senior** who meets the college's dual enrollment admission criteria.
  - TCATs: **any student in grades 9–12**.
- **Deadline:** **June 30**, one application per academic year covering fall, spring and summer.
- **Amounts (the handout says "tentative"):**
  - courses 1–5: up to **$620.55** per 3-credit course (tuition + 5% access fee);
  - courses 6–10: up to **$100 per credit hour**, capped at $600 per course;
  - TCAT: tuition, tiered by clock hours.
- **Limits:** up to **10 courses** at degree-granting institutions; up to **1,296 clock hours** at TCATs.
- **To keep it:** a **2.0 cumulative college GPA** in grant-funded courses. The grant ends when the student falls below 2.0 or completes graduation requirements.

### 4.3 Institution dual enrollment eligibility (examples)

- **MTSU:**
  - junior or senior;
  - ACT, SAT, PSAT, Pre-ACT or Accuplacer scores meeting course cutoffs;
  - **3.0 GPA, or ACT 22**;
  - grant funding only for 1000- and 2000-level courses;
  - $206.85 per credit hour ($620.55 per 3-credit course). **[S18]**
- **UTC ("M.O.C.S. Start"):**
  - finished sophomore year;
  - **3.0 GPA**;
  - courses at the 2000 level or below;
  - "University High School" is a full-time option for Hamilton County 11th and 12th graders. **[S14]**
- **TSU (catalog):**
  - finished sophomore year, with a principal or counselor recommendation and parental permission;
  - ACT 19 composite (English subscore 18 for English courses; Math 19 for math);
  - **and** an index of 100+ (30 × GPA + ACT), **or** 7 required high school units done. **[S29]** ⚠ The same catalog describes an older, smaller dual enrollment grant ($100 per credit, $600 a year) and old tests (PLAN, COMPASS), so parts are outdated.

### 4.4 Other acceleration

- **Middle College Scholarship:** see §3. **[S38]**
- **Move on When Ready:** see §1.5. **[S1]**
- **Early admission (enter college instead of senior year):**
  - ETSU: after 11th grade, **3.5 GPA and ACT 25** per its policy **[S21]**. ⚠ Its requirements page says 2.5 **[S19]**; its freshman page says 3.5 **[S20]**.
  - TSU: 16+ years old, 14 units, **3.2 GPA** in grades 9–11, **ACT 22**, recommendation required. **[S29]**
